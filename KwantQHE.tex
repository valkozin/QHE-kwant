\section{Introduction}

In this project we want to discuss the effect of a cavity on the quantum Hall effect (QHE).

We might discuss the following papers:
\begin{itemize}
	\item \citet{yi-thomasIntegerQuantumHall2025} (Das Sarma):
		It is claimed that even a small temperature $k_B T = 0.05 \hbar \omega_c$ destroys QHE if the sample is perfectly clean.
	\item \citet{arwasQuantumElectronTransport2023}:
		Work on cavity-modified QHE, where they consider a small sample which is much larger than the magnetic localization length and included the cavity perturbatively.
	\item \citet{kimQuantumHallEffect2021}:
		A quasi-particle Wigner crystal (QPWC) results in the persistence of plateaus of finite extent even in the limit of vanishing disorder.
	\item \citet{enknerTunableVacuumfieldControl2025}:
		They demonstrate that adjusting the coupling strength between a two-dimensional electron gas and the vacuum fields of a hovering split-ring resonator leads to a significant reduction in exchange splitting at odd-integer filling factors, along with an enhancement of fractional quantum Hall gaps at filling factors 4/3, 5/3 and 7/5.
\end{itemize}

\section{Paper discussions}

\subsection{Integer Quantum Hall Effect: Disorder, Temperature, Localization, Floating, and Plateau Width}

In Ref.~\cite{yi-thomasIntegerQuantumHall2025} (Das Sarma),
	it is claimed that even a small temperature $k_B T = 0.05 \hbar \omega_c$ destroys QHE if the sample is perfectly clean.
Specifically they mention for disorder strength $\Gamma < k_B T$, that the plateau with in terms of the filling factor $\nu$ is zero (see Fig.~8 in their paper).
We are not sure yet what is going on and need to investigate.

Additionally, we suspect slight differences in the interpretation if the chemical potential is fixed or electron density is fixed.

\subsection{Quantum Electron Transport Controlled by Cavity Vacuum Fields}

In Ref.~\cite{arwasQuantumElectronTransport2023},
	they analyze the cavity-modified QHE, where they consider a small sample which is much larger than the magnetic localization length and included the cavity perturbatively.

We think the magnetic field acts like a local impurity (cyclotron motion) with the length scale
\begin{align}
	l_B = \sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{q_e B}},
\end{align}
where $q_e$ is the elementary charge and $B$ the magnetic flux density. For their parameters in Fig.~5 ($B = 1 \mathrm{T}$) we obtain $l_B = 25.7 \mathrm{nm}$.
They use a system of dimensions $L_x = 500 \mathrm{nm}$ and $L_y = 333 \mathrm{nm}$.
